How it works

Money that stays yours until it's claimed.

Every wallet shares one flaw: a single wrong character in an address and the money is gone for good. Backsy changes what "sent" means, so a mistake becomes something you can take back.

Two ways to send

Before a transfer you pick who you're paying:

  • Protected — for someone you don't know yet. The money moves into an escrow that only you can recover, and the recipient collects it with a link. Until they do, one tap brings it home.
  • Direct — for someone you already trust. Straight into their wallet, instant and final, exactly like any other wallet.

The life of a protected transfer

  1. You send. The funds leave your spendable balance and sit in a one-time escrow.
  2. The recipient gets a claim link. When they open it, the money moves to them and the transfer becomes final.
  3. Changed your mind first? Cancel it and the full amount returns to your balance immediately.
  4. Nobody claimed it? After 7 days the transfer returns to you on its own — no action needed.

A transfer is only ever in one of four states: pending, claimed, cancelled, or expired. Once it leaves pending it can never go back — that's what stops the same money being spent twice.

What the recipient sees

A claim link works in any browser. The recipient doesn't need an account before you send — they can set one up when they claim. If they never do, nothing is lost, because the money was never really theirs yet.

Your balance, honestly labelled

Backsy splits your balance into spendable and in flight. In-flight money is money you've sent but can still recover. It is shown separately so you always know what you can actually spend right now.

Which networks

The current prototype runs on a simulated ledger and does not touch a real chain. Solana is the first target for the on-chain version, since escrow accounts there are cheap enough for everyday amounts.

Backsy is an early prototype. Reversible transfers are demonstrated with a simulated escrow — no on-chain program is deployed and no real funds move.
