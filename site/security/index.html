Security

Control stays on your side.

A reversible transfer is only worth anything if nobody but you can reverse it. This page explains what protects a transfer today, and — just as importantly — what does not yet exist.

Only the sender can cancel

Cancelling is checked on the server against the transfer's own sender, not against whoever clicked the button. A recipient who has your claim link still cannot pull a transfer back to themselves, and neither can a stranger who guesses a link.

A transfer settles exactly once

Claiming and cancelling both operate on a transfer that is still pending, inside a single database transaction. If two claims arrive at the same instant, the first one wins and the second finds nothing left to settle. The same guard makes a double refund impossible.

Balances cannot go negative

Money leaves your balance the moment you send, using a conditional write that simply fails if the funds are not there. Two simultaneous sends cannot overdraw the same account between them.

Claim links are unguessable

Every claim link carries 128 bits of cryptographic randomness. Treat the link itself as the credential: anyone holding it can claim that transfer, so send it over a channel you trust.

Unclaimed money comes home

A transfer nobody collects is not stranded. After 7 days it returns to the sender automatically, so a wrong or abandoned link costs you the wait and nothing else.

Being honest about the risks. This prototype is not self-custodial, holds no keys, and has not been audited. Balances live in an ordinary database on the server, and the escrow is simulated rather than enforced by a smart contract. Do not put real money behind it in its current form.

What real custody would require

Making these guarantees hold without trusting a server means moving the escrow on-chain: a program that holds funds in a per-transfer account, releases them against the claim secret, and lets only the original sender withdraw early. Until that program is deployed and audited, the protection described above is a product promise, not a cryptographic one.

Backsy is an early prototype. Reversible transfers are demonstrated with a simulated escrow — no on-chain program is deployed and no real funds move.
